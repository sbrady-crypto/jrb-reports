Strategic Read

What to do with this

01

IA's edge is real but modest — not the 10–15% Whitney's hypothesis called for

IA beats Standard on cost-per-incremental-conversion by 2.0% and on incremental sales by 4.9% (Meta CLS, 100% confidence on Purchase). That's a genuine edge, but well short of the 10–15% efficiency gain floated pre-test. Treat IA as a marginal, not transformative, upgrade at current scale.

02

Two measurement systems, two different questions — not a contradiction

Meta CLS asks "did the ad cause this?" and says IA wins. Northbeam and GA4 ask "who gets credit for this?" and say Standard wins — more sessions, higher conversion rate, better blended ROAS. This is the exact dynamic flagged in the May 19 ROAS investigation. Don't let Northbeam/GA4 numbers alone drive the IA rollout decision.

03

IA buys reach ~19% cheaper, but converts clicks less efficiently

IA's $32.30 blended CPM (Meta) vs. Standard's $39.83 confirms the pattern Tommy flagged in March. But IA's link CTR (1.24%) trails Standard's (1.44%), and Northbeam's conversion rate is also behind (1.92% vs 2.19%) — IA's cheaper reach is colder, consistent with the View-Through/quality concerns raised in April.

04

VO/CO behavior doesn't change meaningfully by attribution type

CO outperforms VO on cost-efficiency, VO outperforms CO on AOV — and that pattern holds inside both the IA and Standard cells. This answers Tommy's open question from June 5: no evidence yet that VO+IA or CO+IA need a different treatment than VO+Standard or CO+Standard.

05

Recommendation: hold steady, let the test finish, re-read InitiateCheckout and QuizCompletion at close

This pull is ~12.3 of 14 days with primary objectives already at 100% confidence — directionally safe to act on, but InitiateCheckout and QuizCompletion (the two objectives favoring Standard) are worth a final check once the study closes 7/1 before locking in any IA expansion beyond current share.
