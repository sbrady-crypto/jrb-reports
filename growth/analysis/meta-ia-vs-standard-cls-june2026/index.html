Strategic Read

What to do with this

01

IA's edge is real but modest — not the 10–15% Whitney's hypothesis called for

IA beats Standard on cost-per-incremental-conversion by 1.5% and on incremental sales by 6.1% (Meta CLS, 100% confidence on Purchase). That's a genuine edge, but well short of the 10–15% efficiency gain floated pre-test. Treat IA as a marginal, not transformative, upgrade at current scale.

02

The attribution-vs-incrementality gap is the actual story

Meta CLS says IA wins; Northbeam (higher conversion rate, better blended ROAS) and GA4 (more sessions, deeper engagement) both say Standard looks stronger. This is the exact dynamic flagged in the May 19 ROAS investigation — IA volume dilutes blended ROAS optically while still being the more incremental dollar.

03

IA buys reach ~19% cheaper, but converts clicks less efficiently

IA's $32.41 blended CPM (Meta) vs. Standard's $40.13 confirms the pattern Tommy flagged in March. But IA's link CTR (1.22%) trails Standard's (1.43%), and Northbeam's conversion rate is also behind (1.96% vs 2.18%) — IA's cheaper reach is colder, consistent with the View-Through/quality concerns raised in April.

04

VO/CO behavior doesn't change meaningfully by attribution type

CO outperforms VO on cost-efficiency, VO outperforms CO on AOV — and that pattern holds inside both the IA and Standard cells. This answers Tommy's open question from June 5: no evidence yet that VO+IA or CO+IA need a different treatment than VO+Standard or CO+Standard.

05

Recommendation: hold steady, let the test finish, re-read InitiateCheckout at close

This pull is 12.5 of 14 days with primary objectives already at 100% confidence — directionally safe to act on, but InitiateCheckout (favors Standard) and AddPaymentInfo (94.9% confidence on Standard) are worth a final check once the study closes 7/1 before locking in any IA expansion beyond current share.
