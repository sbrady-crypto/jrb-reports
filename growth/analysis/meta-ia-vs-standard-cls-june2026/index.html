Strategic Read

What to do with this

01

IA's edge is real but modest — not the 10–15% Whitney's hypothesis called for

IA beats Standard on CPiC by 1.5% and on incremental sales by 6.1%. That's a genuine, confidently-measured edge (100% confidence on Purchase for both cells) — but well short of the 10–15% efficiency gain floated pre-test. Treat IA as a marginal, not transformative, upgrade at current scale.

02

The attribution-vs-incrementality gap is the actual story

Meta CLS says IA wins; Northbeam and GA4 both say Standard looks stronger (more sessions, higher CVR, better blended ROAS). This is the exact dynamic flagged in the May 19 ROAS investigation — IA volume dilutes blended ROAS optically while still being the more incremental dollar. Don't let Northbeam/GA4 numbers alone drive the IA rollout decision.

03

IA buys reach ~19% cheaper, but converts clicks less efficiently

IA's $32.41 blended CPM vs. Standard's $40.13 confirms the pattern Tommy flagged back in March. But IA's link CTR (1.22%) trails Standard's (1.43%), and on-site CVR is also slightly behind (1.37% vs 1.43%) — IA's cheaper reach is colder, consistent with the View-Through/quality concerns raised in April.

04

VO/CO behavior doesn't change meaningfully by attribution type

CO outperforms VO on cost-efficiency, VO outperforms CO on AOV — and that pattern holds inside both the IA and Standard cells. This answers Tommy's open question from June 5: there's no evidence yet that VO+IA or CO+IA need a different treatment than VO+Standard or CO+Standard.

05

Recommendation: hold steady, let the test finish, re-read at 100% confidence on InitiateCheckout

This pull is 12.5 of 14 days with primary objectives already at 100% confidence — directionally safe to act on, but InitiateCheckout (the one objective favoring Standard) and AddPaymentInfo (94.9% confidence on Standard) are worth a final check once the study closes 7/1 before locking in any IA expansion beyond current share.
